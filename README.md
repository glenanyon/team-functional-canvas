# Team Functional Canvas
> The Team Functional Canvas is a practical framework for describing Team Architecture and improving Team Intelligence by connecting purpose, governance, knowledge, roles, resources and work into a coherent model for people and AI.

## Overview

The **Team Functional Canvas (TFC)** is a structured blueprint for how a team is designed, operates and delivers value. It brings the most important parts of a team into one coherent model so that team members, leaders, stakeholders and authorised AI can work from a consistent understanding.

The TFC is intended for anyone who wants to understand, design, review or improve a team within an organisation.

## Team Intelligence

**Team Intelligence** is the collective capability of a team to understand its situation, make sound decisions, perform work, learn and adapt toward shared outcomes.

It depends on more than individual expertise. It also depends on shared organisational context, including:

- purpose and outcomes;
- scope and boundaries;
- rules and decision authorities;
- stakeholder relationships;
- communication arrangements;
- knowledge and evidence;
- roles and competencies;
- resources and systems; and
- the work the team performs.

When this context is fragmented, undocumented or understood differently by different people, the team relies on assumptions and personal memory. The same limitation affects the safe and useful application of AI.

The TFC makes this context visible, structured and connected.

## The Canvas

The TFC is organised into three connected areas.

### Team Design

Defines what the team is, why it exists and how it operates.

1. **Team Foundation (1.1)**: identity, mandate, services, scope, structure and boundaries.
2. **Team Mission (1.2)**: purpose, outcomes, value drivers, measures of success and strategic intent.
3. **Team Operating System (1.3)**: work flow, operating rhythms, coordination, decisions, constraints and abnormal operations.

### Team Environment

Defines the conditions that shape and constrain the team.

4. **Team Rules (2.1)**: governance, controls, authorities, approvals, obligations and assurance.
5. **Stakeholders (2.2)**: who the team serves, depends upon, influences and affects.
6. **Communication Protocol (2.3)**: how information, consultation, decisions and coordination flow.
7. **Team Knowledge Base (2.4)**: the knowledge, information, terminology, evidence and expertise required by the team.

### Team Delivery

Defines the capability that turns intent into results.

8. **Team Roles (3.1)**: roles, competencies, authorities and human capability.
9. **Team Resources (3.2)**: systems, data, tools, methods, equipment, facilities, services and AI capability.
10. **Team Tasks (3.3)**: the significant work connecting the team’s mission to execution.

## What the TFC Helps You Do

The TFC can help a team or organisation to:

- create a shared description of the team’s purpose, mandate, scope and boundaries;
- connect organisational value and desired outcomes to the work performed;
- clarify roles, decision rights, governance, stakeholders and handovers;
- identify the knowledge, systems, tools and resources required for delivery;
- make gaps, overlaps, constraints, dependencies and single points of failure visible;
- improve onboarding and role clarity;
- support team design and operating-model reviews;
- strengthen governance, assurance and business continuity;
- identify knowledge, capability and resource development needs; and
- provide structured organisational context for responsible AI and automation.

## How to Use the TFC

### 1. Define the team

Agree which team is being described and the organisational level at which the canvas will operate.

### 2. Start with purpose and boundaries

Describe why the team exists, what it provides, what is included in its scope and what is explicitly outside its responsibility.

### 3. Describe how the team works

Capture operating rhythms, work intake, prioritisation, decision arrangements, governance and important interfaces.

### 4. Connect capability to work

Identify the roles, knowledge and resources required to perform the team’s significant tasks.

### 5. Test the connections

Check that:

- desired outcomes have supporting tasks;
- tasks are within the team’s scope;
- every significant task has an accountable owner;
- tasks have the required knowledge and resources;
- relevant controls and authority limits are clear;
- outputs have identified recipients; and
- critical work has continuity, backup or escalation arrangements.

### 6. Validate with the right people

Confirm important assumptions, authorities, obligations and requirements with accountable people and authoritative sources.

### 7. Keep it current

Review the canvas when the team’s purpose, scope, structure, systems, governance, stakeholders or work materially changes.

## What Good Looks Like

A useful TFC is:

- **concise enough to understand**;
- **specific enough to guide decisions**;
- **connected across all ten sections**;
- **grounded in evidence**;
- **clear about assumptions and unknowns**;
- **based on roles rather than named individuals**; and
- **representative of work as it is actually performed**, not only how it is documented.

The TFC is not simply an organisation chart. It is the connected architecture of the team’s purpose, environment, capability and work.

## Example Traceability

A core principle of the TFC is traceability across the canvas:

```text
Organisational Value
        ↑
Desired Outcome
        ↑
Team Task
   ↙    ↓     ↘
Roles  Knowledge  Resources
        ↓
Rules, Evidence and Stakeholder Outputs
```

For example, a task should be traceable to the outcome it supports, the role accountable for it, the knowledge and resources it needs, the controls that govern it and the stakeholder who receives its output.

## Responsible Use

The TFC can support human and AI-assisted work, but it does not independently establish legal obligations, delegated authorities, professional requirements or permission for AI action. High-consequence statements and decisions should be validated against accountable authorities and controlled sources.

AI should augment team capability without obscuring human accountability, weakening controls or treating generated content as authoritative without appropriate validation.
